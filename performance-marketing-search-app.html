The Google Ads ecosystem

The channels

Search — text ads against explicit intent, the highest-intent channel in the stack.

Performance Max — one goal-based campaign spanning every Google surface.

Demand Gen — visually rich ads across YouTube, Discover, and Gmail for mid-funnel discovery.

YouTube & Display — video and banner reach for awareness and remarketing.

The shift underneath

Every one of these channels now runs on the same underlying shift: manual, keyword-level control is being replaced by signal-fed, AI-driven automation that optimizes bids, creative, and targeting continuously.

The marketer's job moves from setting levers to feeding the model good signals and auditing what it does with them.

Search & intent capture

Why Search still anchors the funnel

A search query is a stated intent — the closest thing to a raised hand in digital advertising. Match types (exact, phrase, broad) control how tightly an ad's targeting hugs that stated intent.

Ad Rank still blends bid, Quality Score, and expected impact — but Quality Score itself is increasingly shaped by AI-predicted relevance rather than static keyword-level history.

Broad match, reframed

Broad match paired with smart bidding lets the auction system match queries by meaning rather than string overlap — trading precise control for reach the algorithm can price correctly in real time.

The tradeoff only pays off with tight conversion tracking; broad match on weak signals just buys noisy traffic.

Performance Max campaigns bid toward a single conversion goal across every Google inventory source at once. Marketers supply asset groups and audience signals; the system decides, per auction, which surface and creative combination to serve.

Smart bidding & AI-powered optimization

Target CPA / tROAS

Bids set per-auction against a target cost-per-acquisition or return-on-ad-spend, using real-time signals no manual rule set can match.

Maximize Conversions

Spends the full budget while bidding to get the most conversions the model can find — useful pre-tROAS, when there isn't yet enough data to target a return.

AI Max for Search

Layers AI-expanded query matching, automatically generated assets, and URL expansion on top of existing Search campaigns.

What it needs to work

Accurate, complete conversion signals. Smart bidding optimizes ruthlessly toward whatever it's told to value — garbage conversion data becomes garbage bidding decisions, fast.

Asset generation inside Performance Max

What the system generates

Headlines and descriptions assembled from a brand's assets and landing pages, image and video variants for surfaces the marketer never manually designed for, and auto-generated combinations tested continuously in production.

Keeping it on-brand

Asset group inputs — strong images, video, and copy variety — are the real lever. A thin asset group forces the system to generate more on its own, with less control over the result.

Review generated combinations in Asset Group reporting; pause anything off-brand before it scales.

Event-based optimization for downstream actions

The problem with optimizing on clicks

A click or a form-fill is a weak proxy for revenue. Bidding toward it optimizes for volume of the cheapest, lowest-quality version of that action.

Feeding the model what matters

Offline conversion imports and value-based conversion actions — qualified lead, trial-to-paid, purchase value — let smart bidding optimize toward the event that actually predicts revenue, days or weeks after the click.

The tighter the loop between a real downstream outcome and the signal Google sees, the better the auction-time decisions get.

Demand Gen & YouTube: full-funnel video

Demand Gen

Visually rich, feed-native ads across YouTube in-stream/Shorts, Discover, and Gmail — built for mid-funnel discovery, look-alike audiences, and Lookalike-style AI expansion off a seed list.

YouTube for the full funnel

Awareness formats (bumper, non-skippable) build reach; skippable in-stream drives consideration; and video action campaigns bid directly toward conversions, reusing the same AI bidding stack as Search and PMax.

Mobile app economics run on a different unit than web: cost-per-install buys attention, but Lifetime Value earned after the install is what actually pays for it.

Store presence & App Store Optimization (ASO)

What ASO controls

Title, subtitle, and keyword fields; icon, screenshots, and preview video; ratings and review velocity — the on-store conversion rate from listing view to install.

App Store vs. Google Play

Apple's App Store weights title/subtitle keyword relevance and in-app events; Google Play's algorithm leans more on the long-form description, engagement, and retention signals fed back from install cohorts.

Paid install campaigns and organic ASO compound each other — paid volume feeds the ranking signals that improve organic discovery.

Deep linking

What it solves

A tap on an ad or an email should land the user on the exact in-app screen the creative promised — not the app's generic home screen, which quietly kills conversion.

Deferred deep linking

For a user who doesn't have the app yet, a deferred deep link routes through the store install, then still opens to the right in-app destination on first launch — carrying campaign and content context across the install itself.

Mobile attribution: install vs. in-app events

Install attribution

Which click or impression gets credit for the install — the entry point into every downstream mobile measurement question.

In-app event attribution

The events that follow — registration, first purchase, subscription — matter more for LTV than the install itself, and require their own attribution windows and event-value definitions to optimize campaigns correctly.

A campaign optimized purely on install volume can still be unprofitable if it drives installs that never convert past onboarding.

Attribution models: last-click vs. multi-touch

ModelWhat it creditsWhere it breaks down
Last-clickThe final touchpoint before install/conversionIgnores every upper-funnel channel that built the intent
First-clickThe first touchpoint that introduced the userIgnores everything that closed the conversion
Multi-touch (linear, position-based, time-decay)Credit split across the observed pathOnly as good as the paths it can actually observe
Data-driven / algorithmicModel-learned credit from converting vs. non-converting pathsNeeds volume; opaque to audit at low conversion counts

On mobile, every model above sits on top of a harder problem first: whether the underlying touchpoints can even be observed.

MMPs, SRNs & privacy constraints

Mobile Measurement Partners (MMPs)

Third-party attribution layers (AppsFlyer, Adjust, Branch) that sit between ad networks and the app, deduplicating install credit across channels the way no single network can on its own.

Self-Reporting Networks (SRNs)

Large platforms (Google, Meta, TikTok) that attribute and report their own performance, graded against MMP data for consistency rather than taken as ground truth alone.

SKAdNetwork and Privacy Sandbox push measurement toward aggregated, delayed, and probabilistic signals — deterministic, per-user tracking is no longer the default assumption.

Onboarding, activation psychology & friction reduction

Why the first session decides everything

Most uninstalls and abandonment happen in the first session. Every extra field, permission prompt, or forced sign-up before the user reaches value is a point of drop-off.

Reducing friction without losing signal

Defer account creation until after an "aha" moment, request permissions in context rather than up front, and use progressive profiling to collect data over multiple sessions instead of one long form.

Activation — reaching a defined moment of value — is the metric that predicts retention, not the install itself.

Monetization models, LTV & ROAS

IAA (In-App Advertising)

Revenue from ads shown inside the app — scales with engaged time, not purchase intent.

IAP & subscriptions

One-time in-app purchases and recurring subscriptions — higher revenue per user, more sensitive to onboarding friction.

Off-app monetization

Value captured outside the app itself — e-commerce purchases, in-store visits, or web conversions the app influenced.

LTV & ROAS

Lifetime Value predicts what a cohort is worth over time; Return on Ad Spend compares that value against acquisition cost — the two numbers that decide whether a campaign should scale.

A single user journey now spans every one of these channels. AI-agent coordination sequences the next-best message across all of them — suppressing a push notification if an email already landed, or triggering retargeting only after in-app nudges have failed to re-engage.

Agentic campaign management

What AI agents do here

Continuous, real-time auditing of spend and pacing, anomaly detection on conversion rate or cost drops, and optimization recommendations surfaced faster than a weekly manual review could catch them.

Where humans stay in control

Budget ceilings, brand and creative decisions, and strategic priorities remain human calls. Agents flag and recommend; they don't get standing authority to reallocate budget or ship creative without a review gate.

The value is catching a problem in hours instead of days — not removing the marketer from the loop.

Best practices

1. Feed smart bidding accurate, complete conversion signals before trusting it with budget
2. Keep asset groups rich — the input quality bounds what Performance Max can generate
3. Optimize toward downstream value events, not clicks or form-fills alone
4. Pair paid install campaigns with ASO — each compounds the other's signal
5. Use deferred deep links so paid traffic lands on the promised in-app screen
6. Cross-check SRN-reported results against MMP data rather than trusting either alone
7. Design onboarding around the first-session activation moment, not feature completeness
8. Size campaigns on LTV and ROAS, never cost-per-install in isolation
9. Sequence push, email, SMS, and retargeting so channels suppress rather than duplicate each other
10. Let AI agents flag anomalies and recommend — keep budget, brand, and priority calls human
