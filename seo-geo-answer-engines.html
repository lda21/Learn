SEO, GEO & Answer-Engine Optimization

From ranking on a results page to being the answer — search foundations, Generative Engine Optimization, and agentic workflows for the LLM era

Search hasn't ended — it's split in two

Traditional search

A ranked list of blue links a person scans, clicks, and evaluates themselves. Success is measured by position and click-through.

Answer engines

AI Overviews, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini synthesize an answer from your content and may never send a click at all. Success is measured by citation and inclusion.

Both channels read the same web — but they reward different things, so the discipline that wins one doesn't automatically win the other.

Crawlability: the foundation under everything

What has to be true

A crawler — Googlebot or an LLM's fetcher — has to be able to reach a page, render it, and parse it before anything else matters.

robots.txt directives, canonical tags, and a clean XML sitemap are the plumbing; get them wrong and great content never gets indexed.

Where it silently breaks

Content gated behind client-side JavaScript that some crawlers don't execute, orphaned pages with no internal links pointing to them, and infinite-scroll content with no crawlable pagination.

Fix: server-side render or pre-render critical content, and audit crawl logs, not just rankings.

Core Web Vitals

LCP

Largest Contentful Paint — how fast the main content renders. Target under 2.5s.

INP

Interaction to Next Paint — how responsive the page feels to input. Target under 200ms.

CLS

Cumulative Layout Shift — how much content jumps around while loading. Target under 0.1.

Why it still matters

A confirmed ranking signal for traditional search, and a proxy for page quality that crawlers of all kinds correlate with trustworthy content.

Structured data

What it does

Schema.org markup (via JSON-LD) explicitly labels what a page's content means — this is a Product, this is a Recipe, this is an FAQ — instead of leaving it to inference.

Why it matters more now

Explicit, machine-readable facts are exactly what LLM-based answer engines prefer to extract over ambiguous prose. Clean schema is a direct line into an AI-generated answer.

Topic clusters & intent mapping

Topic clusters

One authoritative pillar page covering a broad topic, linked to and from a set of narrower cluster pages that each go deep on one subtopic.

Intent mapping

Every query carries an intent — informational, navigational, commercial, or transactional. Content that answers the wrong intent for its target query won't rank, no matter how well it's written.

Clusters built around real intent, not just keyword volume, are what both classic rankings and AI answer selection reward.

SignalWhat it demonstrates
ExperienceFirst-hand use or direct involvement, not secondhand summary
ExpertiseDepth and accuracy that a generalist couldn't produce
AuthoritativenessRecognition by other credible sources — citations, backlinks, mentions
TrustworthinessAccurate, transparent, and safe — author bylines, sourcing, no dark patterns

E-E-A-T isn't a direct ranking factor with a formula — it's the underlying quality bar that ranking systems and answer engines are both trying to approximate.

Enter GEO: Generative Engine Optimization

SEO's job

Earn a high position in a ranked list a human will scan.

GEO's job

Earn a place inside the synthesized answer itself — get cited, get quoted, get referenced when a model composes a response.

GEO doesn't replace SEO — a page still has to be crawlable, fast, and well-structured to be eligible. GEO is what happens on top of that foundation to win the citation, not just the click.

Entity-based optimization

Keywords vs. entities

Classic SEO optimized for strings — the exact keyword phrase. LLMs reason over entities — people, organizations, products, concepts — and the relationships between them.

What to actually do

Name entities explicitly and consistently, link them to canonical references (Wikipedia, Wikidata, official docs), and use structured data to disambiguate which "Claude" or "Apollo" a page means.

A page that's unambiguous about what and who it's discussing is easier for a model to place correctly in its answer.

llms.txt

What it is

A proposed convention — a plain-Markdown file at /llms.txt — that gives AI crawlers a concise, curated map of a site's most important content, since LLM context windows can't ingest an entire site the way a search index can.

Where it stands today

Not a universally adopted standard the way robots.txt is — support varies by crawler. Treat it as a low-cost bet: cheap to publish, potentially high-leverage as adoption grows.

Direct-answer patterns

What answer engines extract

A tight, self-contained sentence or short paragraph that directly answers a question — placed near the top, not buried under three paragraphs of preamble.

Formats that extract cleanly

Explicit Q&A headers, numbered steps, definition-style opening sentences ("X is …"), and comparison tables — structures a model can lift with minimal rewriting.

Write the answer first, the nuance after — the inverse of a lot of legacy SEO advice to build up context before the payoff.

The answer-engine landscape

EngineWhat it favors
Google AI OverviewsPages that already rank well organically; heavy reliance on the existing index
ChatGPT SearchFresh, well-cited web results blended with Bing-style indexing
PerplexityExplicit inline citations; rewards clearly sourced, fact-dense pages
ClaudeWeb search grounded in cited sources; favors precise, well-structured answers
GeminiDeep integration with Google's index and knowledge graph entities

Each engine's citation behavior differs — optimizing for one alone risks under-indexing for the others.

Measuring GEO performance

Old metrics don't transfer cleanly

Rank position and organic click volume don't capture a "zero-click" answer where a brand was cited but no visit occurred.

What to track instead

Citation frequency — how often a brand appears in AI-generated answers for target queries, sampled with prompt panels run on a schedule, plus referral traffic tagged from AI-answer surfaces where available.

This category of tooling is immature and changing fast — treat any GEO analytics platform as directional, not authoritative.

AI agents can now run a large share of the SEO/GEO workflow autonomously — crawling a site, diagnosing issues, and drafting fixes — but publishing decisions stay behind a human review gate.

Automating content audits

What an agent can check

Missing or malformed structured data, thin or duplicate content, broken internal links, stale statistics, and missing direct-answer summaries — all at a scale no manual audit matches.

Where a human stays in the loop

Judging whether a rewrite preserves brand voice and factual accuracy, and deciding which fixes are worth the effort versus low-priority noise.

Agentic topic & entity analysis

Topic gap detection

Agents can compare a site's coverage against a competitor set and a live query landscape, surfacing subtopics with real demand and no owned page.

Entity consistency checks

Scanning a whole site for entity names used inconsistently, missing schema linkage, or ambiguous references that could confuse an extracting model.

The value isn't replacing strategic judgment — it's running the exhaustive, repetitive analysis that made comprehensive audits impractical to do by hand.

Agentic performance monitoring

What to automate

Scheduled Core Web Vitals checks, rank-tracking on priority queries, and periodic AI-answer citation sampling — run continuously instead of in occasional manual sweeps.

Closing the loop

Route anomalies — a Vitals regression, a citation drop-off, a sudden ranking loss — into an alert an owner actually sees, not a dashboard nobody opens.

Best practices

1. Fix crawlability and Core Web Vitals before investing in content — nothing else matters if pages aren't reachable
2. Ship complete, valid structured data on every page type it applies to, not just the flagship pages
3. Lead with a direct, self-contained answer before the supporting nuance
4. Name entities explicitly and link them to canonical references
5. Publish and maintain an llms.txt as a low-cost, high-upside bet
6. Track citation frequency across multiple answer engines, not just one
7. Let agents handle exhaustive audits; keep humans on judgment calls and publishing
8. Revisit topic clusters and intent maps quarterly — query behavior shifts fast
9. Keep E-E-A-T signals — real authorship, sourcing, transparency — visible on the page itself
10. Treat SEO and GEO as one shared foundation with two different scorecards, not two competing programs
