From Channel Manager to Workflow Architect

Channel Manager (2015–2022)

  • Owns one surface: the Google Ads UI plus a weekly CPC / CPL deck
  • Email in Marketo or Braze, social in the scheduler — the deliverable is a send
  • Graded on channel-reported ROAS — the platform grading itself
  • Failure mode: the asset shipped late, or CPC rose

Workflow Architect (2023–2026)

  • Owns a directed graph across DATA → MEDIA → CONTENT → PRODUCT
  • GA4 + Snowplow → BigQuery → dbt fct_sessions and an ICP-fit score
  • Hightouch reverse-ETL → Meta CAPI, Google Customer Match, Customer.io
  • CRM writes opportunity stage back; that write-back retrains the score
  • Failure mode: a sync fails silently at 03:00, unpaged

Five deliverables that replace “the campaign”:

  • Data contract — event names, properties, owner, schema version
  • Runbook — what fires, on what trigger, with what fallback
  • SLA — latency, freshness, who gets paged
  • Eval set — 20–40 golden examples with expected outputs
  • Context pack — ICP, positioning, objections, banned claims, brand voice

The tell: a channel manager asks “what is our ROAS on Meta?” An architect asks “pause Meta in eight matched DMAs — what happens to blended MER?” — the org shape trends toward fewer, more senior practitioners plus agents rather than a bench of channel generalists; treat any specific headcount ratio you see quoted as a vendor or consultancy claim with no published method (slide 46 returns to this). New titles since 2022: Marketing AI Operations Lead, Growth Engineer, Prompt & Workflow Designer, AI Content Quality Editor.

Ten Formulas You Will Be Quizzed On

1. CAC

CAC = fully-loaded S&M spend / new customers

Media, salaries, agency, tools, content. Media-only gets you fired.

2. Blended vs paid-isolated CAC

blended = total spend / all new customers

Blended flatters with organic; state which you mean.

3. CAC payback

months = CAC / (ARPA × GM%)

Gross-margin adjusted or fiction; 12–18 months is folklore.

4. LTV

LTV = (ARPA × GM%) / monthly churn

Revenue instead of gross profit inflates LTV:CAC by 1 / GM.

5. LTV:CAC

LTV : CAC, healthy 3:1–5:1

Below 3:1 you buy revenue; above 6:1 you under-invest.

6. Pipeline coverage

coverage = open pipeline / quota

Teams target 3–5x; principled is 1 / win rate.

7. Pipeline velocity

(opps × ACV × win rate) / cycle days

Dollars per day — names the lever: volume, price, conversion, speed.

8. Win rate

win rate = won / (won + lost)

Decide in writing whether “no decision” is a loss.

9. MER vs ROAS

MER = revenue / total spend; ROAS = platform revenue / platform spend

MER cannot double-count; ROAS can.

10. Break-even MER

break-even = 1 / contribution margin

A 20%-margin brand clears 5.0x; 50%-margin clears 2.0x.

Attribution windows are part of the formula: Meta 7-day click / 1-day view, Google Ads 30-day click, GA4 data-driven — never compare unnormalised. Board layer: NRR (120%+, Bessemer), Rule of 40 (Brad Feld, 2015: growth% + FCF margin% ≥ 40), the Magic Number.

Sourced vs Influenced

  • Sourced — marketing created the contact record and the opportunity
  • Influenced — any marketing touch; lands at 80–95%, so unfalsifiable as a commitment
  • Define both, then carry exactly one as the target

What the CFO Actually Asks

  • Fully-loaded CAC — rising or falling quarter over quarter?
  • Payback period against cash runway
  • Incremental return on the last dollar, not the average
  • Which spend is cancellable if we cut 20% in-quarter?

Close the loop on budget: 13,235 leads at a $150 blended CPL is ~$2.0M of media, ~$3.2M fully loaded. At a 30% win rate the 240 opportunities become ~72 logos, so marketing-sourced CAC is ~$44K. Against $50K ACV at 80% gross margin (~$3,333/month) that is a 13.3-month payback, inside an 18-month bar.

Timing trap: spend is cash out this month; revenue books 3–9 months later. Cohort spend against the pipeline it created, never against same-month revenue.

B2C Translation: Verano Coffee Runs the Same Machine

The B2C / DTC translation: the worked example switches to Verano Coffee, a fictional DTC subscription; the rest of Module 1 stays on Lumen Payroll.

What Replaces the Demand Waterfall

  • AARRR (Dave McClure, 500 Startups, 2007): Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue
  • Renderings swap the last two — write the stages, not a sequence
  • Same discipline: machine-checkable entry criterion, owner, reversible transition
  • No MQL / SAL / SQL and no human accept step, so slide 3's reason-code data never exists
  • Substitutes: return / refund reason and cancellation reason, as structured fields

What Replaces Pipeline Coverage

  • The binding constraint is contribution margin, not coverage
  • Control metric: break-even MER = 1 / contribution margin
  • Verano: AOV £38, 22% contribution margin after COGS, fulfilment and fees → break-even MER 4.55x
  • So 3.0x blended MER at Verano is a loss dressed as growth
  • Blended vs paid-isolated CAC is the DTC board argument — two different metrics

Same chain, B2C shape: £4M / £38 AOV = 105,263 orders; 60% come from returning customers, so 42,105 new customers; / 2.8% site conversion = ~1.5M sessions. Spend ceiling = 42,105 × allowable CAC (first-order contribution plus churn-discounted repeat).

Trap: sizing budget off LTV before the cohort has aged. Under 6–12 months, an LTV-justified CAC is a forecast spent as cash.

B2C has far more events and no sales team, so AI leverage sits in creative volume, lifecycle triggers and incrementality testing.

Demand Capture, Demand Creation, and the 66-Point Attribution Gap

Capture harvests demand that creation manufactured; scoring both on click attribution defunds creation.

Demand capture — they already know

  • Branded and non-brand high-intent search
  • G2, Capterra, TrustRadius comparison pages
  • Retargeting, /alternatives and /vs pages, affiliate
  • Lag: hours. ROAS: high. Ceiling: hard
  • Double branded-search budget: nothing happens

Demand creation — they do not know yet

  • Podcast, LinkedIn organic and executive posting, YouTube
  • Community, events, original research, newsletters, PR
  • Lag: weeks. Click-measured ROAS: near zero. Ceiling: none
  • No UTM at the moment of decision

The measurement gap, with the actual numbers

  • Refine Labs hybrid-attribution study, April 2023: n = 620 declared-intent conversions, $21.5M closed-won ARR
  • Software credited web search 78% of demand creation; buyers self-reported 12% — a 66-point gap
  • Self-report attributed 85% of conversions to dark social (social, podcast, word of mouth, community)
  • Closed-won: software said 79% web search, customers said 3% — a 76-point gap
  • Podcast alone carried 53% of self-reported closed-won revenue ($11.4M) against 0% in software attribution
  • One agency’s sample, not an industry study

Dark social — the Slack link, the podcast in the car — carries no UTM, so branded search takes credit weeks later. Fix: a required free-text HDYHAU field on high-intent forms, LLM-classified nightly into the CRM. 95:5 rule (John Dawes, Ehrenberg-Bass, via LinkedIn’s B2B Institute): ~5% of category buyers are in-market per quarter, derived from an assumed ~5-year purchase cycle against a ~90-day sales cycle — a planning heuristic, not a measured constant.

Triangulation: Three Methods, Three Questions — Plus What the Buyer Says

Why last-click stopped working

  • Safari ITP, Firefox ETP and iOS ATT killed cookie- and device-based tracking
  • AI Overviews and chat assistants answer without a click
  • Last-click credits exactly zero upstream of itself

Platform / multi-touch

  • Which ad do I pause?
  • Daily
  • Bias: Meta and Google both claim the same order
  • Never for cross-channel allocation

Incrementality

  • Is this channel causing revenue?
  • Quarterly, one channel
  • Bias: costs revenue during the holdout; compute power first
  • Never a daily signal

MMM

  • Split next quarter, offline included?
  • Quarterly refresh
  • Bias: needs ~2–3 years of weekly spend and revenue
  • Never for creative or keyword calls

Self-reported (HDYHAU) — adjunct signal

  • What does the buyer say happened?
  • Continuous
  • Bias: recall and non-response
  • Never your only number

Mechanics

  • Designs: geo holdout / matched-market (4–6 weeks dark), PSA or ghost-ads control, platform lift studies
  • Expect branded search and retargeting far below reported ROAS — the finding, not a bug
  • MMM: time-series regression with adstock (carryover) and saturation curves
  • Open-source: google/meridian (Bayesian, 29 January 2025), facebookexperimental/Robyn (Ridge, Weibull adstock, Hill saturation), PyMC-Marketing
  • Managed: Recast, Prescient AI. Meridian GeoX only previewed 5 May 2026
  • The loop: incrementality results become Bayesian priors in the MMM
  • When legs disagree: incrementality wins causality, MMM allocation, platform attribution nothing but intra-channel tuning

Self-report is not a fourth leg — but only it surfaces dark social, podcast and word of mouth. Use it to generate the hypothesis you test with incrementality, never to allocate budget.

Identity Resolution: The Five Joins That Break

Three keys, one profile

anonymous_id → user_id → account_id (company, keyed on email domain). Resolution collapses the three into one profile and backfills the anonymous history the moment the visitor identifies. Almost every “the data is wrong” escalation is one of these joins failing — boring, specific, fixable.

1 Host-only cookie. GCLID set on www, form on go. Fix: cookie on .example.com.
2 Last-touch overwrite. Both touches in one CRM field. Fix: store both, plus a JSON touch array.
3 Personal vs work email. Two contacts, one human. Fix: fuzzy match, then roll up to account.
4 No B2B account rollup. Six people, one deal, six MQLs. Fix: route on the account owner.
5 Form fill without consent state. Not legally activatable. Fix: consent is a required event column.
6 The discipline. Identity logic lives in dbt — versioned, tested, reviewable — not a workflow builder.

Where AI actually earns its place here

Normalising free-text company names (Acme Corp. / ACME / acme inc) into one entity; classifying job titles into personas; de-duplicating records; classifying the HDYHAU free text from slide 8. High-volume, low-stakes classification with human-reviewable output — the ideal first AI workflow, worth more than another ad-copy generator. Module 2 builds the classifier; Module 3 schedules it.

The Product Contract: PQLs and the Instrumentation Spec

The PQL

A user past a behavioural threshold signalling purchase intent: frequency × breadth of adoption × depth.

Lumen Payroll’s definition:

PQL := invited 2+ teammates
   AND 60% of free-tier
       employee limit
   AND core payroll action
       5x in 7 days

Worth several times an MQL: behaviour inside the product, not a form.

Pipeline: Amplitude / Mixpanel / PostHog → BigQuery → dbt PQL model → Hightouch or Fivetran Activations → Salesforce + Customer.io. Pocus and Correlated sell the last mile.

Instrumentation spec: a marketing deliverable

Agreed before the sprint. It names events, properties, identity keys and owner.

  • No workspace_invite_sent event, no campaign triggering on it — why lifecycle ships late
  • Naming convention (object_action, snake_case) and a tracking plan in version control
  • Feature flags coordinated with campaign timing; never announce what is flagged off
  • Beta-cohort access for launch case studies
  • A publicly committable GA date

PLG loops worth instrumenting

  • Viral loop — k-factor and cycle time: k 0.4 at 3 days beats 0.7 at 40
  • Content loop — user pages acquire traffic that acquires users
  • Usage → upgrade — the limit the free tier puts in the path
  • 2026 consensus: PLG has converged into product-led sales — humans triggered by product signals

Anti-pattern: scoring on events product is about to rename. Fix: schema contracts, a deprecation policy, and dbt tests that fail when an event’s volume hits zero — a rename does not error, it silently stops pipeline.

The AI-Native Marketer’s Actual Day

Your output shifts from artifacts to systems, and review shifts from “do I like it” to “does it pass the eval”.

Before — the artifact

  • Ad copy: 12 hand-written variants, upload, wait
  • Reporting: weekly numbers into a Monday deck
  • Briefing: a 6-page brief emailed to an agency
  • Research: skim ten G2 reviews
  • Analysis: request a query, wait three days
  • Experimentation: two A/B tests a quarter

After — the system

  • Ad copy: brand-voice doc, banned-claims list, 30-example golden set, 200 variants. The deliverable is the eval set.
  • Reporting: a scheduled agent queries BigQuery overnight, flags anomalies against a forecast band, posts hypotheses to Slack.
  • Briefing: one context pack in version control — ICP, positioning, proof, objections, tone, banned claims.
  • Research: transcripts, tickets, reviews and lost-deal reasons in the warehouse; cluster objections by frequency × deal value.
  • Analysis: natural-language querying over a governed dbt semantic layer — which must exist first.
  • Experimentation: more tests, so pre-registered hypotheses, minimum detectable effect computed in advance, holdouts.

The five new non-negotiables

(1) version control for prompts, reviewed like code; (2) an eval before any generative step ships; (3) a human gate on anything customer-facing at scale; (4) a monitor on every automated sync, because a dead sync looks like a quiet week; (5) a written statement of what the agent may not do.

Instrument your own brief-to-live cycle time for a quarter before and after. Do not inherit a percentage from a vendor deck.

Classical ML Is Still the Profit Centre

The five models that move revenue are all pre-generative. You brief and challenge them; you do not build them.

1Propensity / lead scoring — P(convert within 30 days). Judged on AUC-ROC and lift at decile 1.
2Churn — P(cancel next cycle). Judged on precision@k. Predicting churn is not preventing it.
3LTV / CLV — predicted 12–24 month contribution margin, fed to bidding as pLTV.
4Lookalikes — nearest-neighbour search over an embedded seed list. Seed quality dominates seed size.
5MMM — Bayesian regression of sales on spend, with adstock and saturation curves.
6Governance — every one needs a drift check and an incrementality test.

Reading the numbers

  • AUC-ROC 0.5 is a coin flip; above ~0.75 is usable.
  • Route the top two deciles to SDRs; slide 15’s reason codes retrain the model.
  • Churn needs uplift modelling (treatment vs control), or you pay customers who would have stayed.
  • Feed pLTV to Google tROAS and Meta Max Conversion Value, not raw purchase value.
  • A seed of 1,000–5,000 best customers beats 100,000 “all customers”.

MMM and governance

  • Google Meridian, open-sourced 29 January 2025: Bayesian, geo-level, successor to LightweightMMM.
  • Alternatives: Meta Robyn, PyMC-Marketing. Cookieless — which is why MMM returned after ATT.
  • Drift check = PSI (Population Stability Index) on the input features.
  • Incrementality = geo holdout or PSA ghost ads. Without both, the dashboard is decoration.

None of these writes a sentence, and none is replaceable by a prompt. When a vendor pitches “AI lead scoring”, ask for the AUC and the holdout.

  • Cannot count characters: validate “30 characters or fewer” in code.
  • Use for variant generation, JSON extraction, classification, call summarisation.

Diffusion — denoising from noise

Prompt in, image or video out.

  • Non-deterministic by design — same prompt, different image without a fixed seed.
  • Fails by drift: hands, logos, product geometry, on-image text.
  • Use for mood boards, background variation, localisation, storyboards.

Reading beats generating

A multimodal LLM takes image, video and PDF in and returns text: “tabulate offer, price anchor and CTA across 60 competitor screenshots”. Understanding pays better than generation — auditable and linear.

The prompting does not transfer

No instruction hierarchy, no system prompt, no tool use — CO-STAR and chain-of-thought do not carry over. Its grammar: subject + medium + composition + lighting + lens + style + negative prompt. Diffusion ships a negative-prompt field; LLM constraints go in the system instruction as a banned list (slide 24).

Rights caveat — a legal exposure

Generated likenesses, trademarks and “in the style of [living artist]” are exposure. Under EU AI Act Article 50, the deployer duties in 50(3) and 50(4) bite marketing: synthetic imagery that makes a product look unlike reality, digital replicas of real people, de-aging of talent, and disclosing that a chatbot is AI. The 50(2) machine-readable marking binds the provider, your vendor. Applies from 2 August 2026; not deferred by the Digital Omnibus.

Chain-of-Thought Has an Expiry Date

“Think step by step” was a 2022 workaround. On 2026 reasoning models it is redundant at best, expensive always, and occasionally harmful.

The Context Window Is a Budget, Not a Target

A 1M-token window is a credit limit. Models degrade long before you fill it; choose what you do not send.

Lost in the middle

Liu et al., 2023, arXiv:2307.03172. Accuracy peaks at the start and end and sags in the middle, even in long-context models.

Context rot

Chroma, 2025, trychroma.com/research/context-rot. All 18 frontier models tested degrade as input grows, before the advertised limit.

Consequence: instruction at the top, critical facts at the bottom, bulk in the middle. Retrieve 5 great chunks, not 200.

Long context

  • Under ~50k tokens, rarely changing, needed on every request.
  • Cacheable, nearly free at volume; you pay on uncached calls.
  • Fails quietly as the pack outgrows what anyone re-reads.

RAG

  • Large corpus, changing often, or a different slice per request.
  • Buys freshness and citability; costs retrieval engineering.
  • Answers from the least-bad chunk: no relevance floor.

Fine-tuning

  • A form prompting will not hold; 500+ examples; stable task.
  • Buys output shape and vocabulary; costs labour, and goes stale.
  • Fails when used to add facts: it does not, and does not update.

Retrieval hygiene

  • Chunk on semantic boundaries; deduplicate; tag source and date.
  • Re-rank after vector search: similarity is not relevance.
  • Set a relevance floor so no good chunk returns nothing.

Agent caveat (Module 3): on a long run the window fills with tool output nobody needs again. The fixes are architectural (compaction, clearing stale results, writing findings to file), not promptable.

How caching works, and how marketers break it

It is a prefix match: any byte change invalidates everything after it. Render order is tools → system → messages.

  • A timestamp or Date.now() anywhere in the system prompt.
  • A per-user name or session ID at the top instead of the bottom.
  • A tool list that varies per user, or unsorted JSON.
  • Switching models mid-run: caches are model-scoped.
  • Bursty traffic against a 5-minute TTL, so gaps re-bill the write premium.

Rule: freeze the system prompt, put volatile tokens after the breakpoint, verify with the cache-read counter.

Claude: cache_control {"type": "ephemeral"} on a system block, optional "ttl": "1h", at most 4 breakpoints. Thinking tokens bill as output (slide 25).

Brand Voice at Scale: Spec → Exemplars → Judge → Regression

Brand voice is only consistent when it is testable.

Spec, pairs, exemplars

  • Voice spec (slide 27): attributes in priority order, POV, tense, reading band, banned words.
  • Priority order decides which attribute wins.
  • Do / don’t pairs: 8–15, same meaning, one off-voice.
  • Exemplars: 3–8 published assets per format; a weak opener teaches one.

Rubric and judge

  • Rubric: spec criteria as 1–5 scales, anchored at 1, 3, 5.
  • LLM-as-judge: Zheng et al., 2023, arXiv:2306.05685; G-Eval (Liu et al., 2023) adds chain-of-thought.
  • Correct for position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias.
  • Calibrate on ~50 human-scored samples; report the agreement rate.

The eval ladder

  • (a) golden set, frozen; (b) rule checks plus judge, reported per criterion.
  • (c) 20 blind human reviews per release; (d) A/B, pre-registered sample.
  • Claim-extraction pass: each claim mapped to a source line; regex for prices, superlatives.

Regression and localisation

  • 30–50 frozen briefs, re-run on every prompt change, model swap or update.
  • Silent model upgrades cause voice drift: the failure nobody plans for.
  • Localisation: translate the spec, not the copy; regenerate per locale, review natively.

      headers: { 'x-goog-api-key': key, 'Api-Revision': '2026-05-20' },
      payload: JSON.stringify({
        model: 'gemini-3.7-flash',
        system_instruction: VOICE,
        input: `Segment: ${segment}\nOffer: ${offer}`,
        generation_config: { thinking_level: 'low' }
      })
    });
    if (res.getResponseCode() !== 200) {                      // fail loud
      sh.getRange(i + 2, 4).setValue('ERR ' + res.getResponseCode());
      return;
    }

    const text = JSON.parse(res.getContentText()).steps
      .filter(s => s.type === 'model_output')                 // NOT every step
      .flatMap(s => s.content || [])
      .filter(c => c.type === 'text')
      .map(c => c.text).join('');

    GmailApp.createDraft('', `[${segment}] draft`, text);     // DRAFT, not send
    sh.getRange(i + 2, 4).setValue('drafted');
  });
}

Four details

  2. The status column makes reruns idempotent.
  3. muteHttpExceptions plus an ERR cell stops silent failure.
  4. Filter on model_output before flattening, or a thought step reaches the draft.

Sheets as a Database — and the Four Ceilings

A Sheet is a fine campaign database until one of four hard ceilings hits. All four are knowable in advance.

10,000,000 cells

Per spreadsheet, not per tab. Ceiling of 18,278 columns. No row cap — a 250-column sheet dies near 40,000 rows.

6 minutes per execution

Runtime limit, consumer and Workspace alike. The one that bites. Fix: batch N rows, a lastRow property, a 5-minute trigger.

Consumer vs Workspace quotas

UrlFetch 20,000 vs 100,000/day. Trigger runtime 90 min vs 6 hr/day. Recipients 100 vs 1,500/day. Per user, 24h reset.

The obligations ceiling

Row-level access control, concurrent writers, shared schema edits, or PII you must delete on request. A Sheet has none.

Graduation signal: hit any of the four and the next stop is BigQuery or a real database behind Cloud Run — not a bigger Sheet. Module 1’s layering restated: governed joins belong in the warehouse layer, not the activation layer’s scratch pad.

Five Ways Vibe-Coded Marketing Apps Fail

Old, well-understood bugs — reproduced at speed and dressed as production-ready.

1. Unreviewed generated code. Scan rates disagree by method. Counter: a human reads it before any public form ships.
2. Secrets in the client bundle. A VITE_ variable is inlined at build time. Counter: server-side proxy only.

Choosing the regime

Distance is set by speed of harm and reversibility — the slide 21 blast-radius rule, stated for governance — never by what the agent can technically do.

  • Email blast — fast, irreversible → gate it
  • Draft in a doc — slow, reversible → do not gate it
  • Bid change inside a hard cap → supervise, do not approve each one
  • HITL does not scale past a few hundred actions a day; HOTL is where mature automation lands

Cite the right document

  • Human involvement must be meaningful: authority and competence to change the decision
  • WP251rev.01 (Article 29 WP, EDPB-endorsed 25 May 2018): no escaping Article 22 “by fabricating human involvement”
  • CJEU SCHUFA (C-634/21, 7 December 2023): a score is itself an Article 22 decision when acted on
  • Do not cite EDPB Opinion 28/2024 — adopted 17 December 2024, it answers model-anonymity and legitimate-interest questions
  • Lead scoring usually sits outside Article 22; the test is whether anything consequential follows automatically

Translation: a human clicking “approve” on 400 items in ten minutes is not oversight — it is liability plus false assurance. Failure mode: gates placed where they are easy (drafts) instead of where they are dangerous (spend, audience, claims).
