Measurement planning

Start from the business question

A measurement plan begins with the decisions the business needs to make — where to shift budget, which channel to scale — and works backward to the events, dimensions, and KPIs that answer them.

The measurement plan artifact

A living document mapping business objective → KPI → GA4 event → parameters → owner. Built before any tagging work starts, so implementation has a spec instead of a guess.

Skipping this step is the single most common cause of an analytics setup that tracks everything and answers nothing.

GTM implementation

Tags, triggers & variables

Google Tag Manager fires tags (what to send) on triggers (when to send it), using variables (values pulled from the page or the dataLayer) — all editable without a code deploy.

The dataLayer contract

A structured JavaScript object that developers push events and parameters into, and GTM reads from — the agreed contract between engineering and analytics that keeps tagging stable across site changes.

GA4 analysis fundamentals

Event-based model

Every interaction is an event with parameters, not a rigid pageview/session hierarchy — sessions and engagement are derived, not primary.

Explorations

Free-form, funnel, path, and cohort exploration techniques for ad-hoc analysis beyond the standard reports.

BigQuery export

Raw, unsampled event-level data exported daily (or streaming) into BigQuery — the foundation for anything AI-assisted below.

Predictive metrics

GA4's native purchase probability and churn probability scores, built on the same event stream, ready to feed audiences and campaigns.

AI-assisted coding & interpretation with Gemini

Gemini in BigQuery

Natural-language prompts generate SQL against the GA4 export schema, explain existing queries, and suggest next questions — collapsing the gap between "I have a question" and "I have a query."

Gemini in Looker & Sheets

Conversational analytics over dashboards and connected sheets: ask for a trend, a breakdown, or an anomaly explanation in plain language and get a chart or summary back.

The AI drafts the query and the narrative — the analyst still owns whether the SQL matches the actual business question.

Human-validated marketing decisions

Where the loop closes

AI-generated SQL, summaries, and forecasts are a draft, not a decision. Before an insight moves budget or changes a campaign, a human checks it against context the model doesn't have.

What validation actually checks

Does the query match the stated question? Is the sample size large enough to act on? Does the result contradict something the team already knows to be true, and if so, why?

The same shift as the measurement workflow: AI does the first pass on volume and pattern-finding, a human decides what the pattern means.

Predicting churn & upsell propensity

Churn propensity

Models trained on engagement decay, support ticket volume, usage-feature breadth, and billing signals to output a per-account probability of churn within a defined window — ranked, not binary.

Upsell propensity

The mirror model: usage hitting plan limits, feature adoption patterns matching past upgraders, and seat growth, scored to surface accounts ready for an expansion conversation before they ask.

Scoring accounts & detecting anomalies

Account scoring

Fit and engagement signals combined into a single score used to prioritize outreach, renewal risk review, or expansion motion — the same scoring logic behind lead scoring, applied post-sale.

Anomaly detection

Statistical or ML models flag metric movements that fall outside expected variance — a traffic drop, a conversion rate spike, a spend anomaly — before a human would notice scanning a dashboard.

Generating hypotheses & narratives

Hypothesis generation

Given an anomaly or a trend, AI proposes candidate explanations drawn from correlated signals in the data — a starting list for the analyst to test, not a verdict.

Narrative generation

Turning a table of numbers into a plain-language summary: what changed, by how much, and what it's correlated with — the first draft of the story a stakeholder actually reads.

Automating executive briefings

What gets automated

Pulling the week's key metrics, flagging anomalies, drafting the narrative, and assembling a briefing document or slide — on a schedule, without an analyst manually rebuilding it every cycle.

What stays manual

The final read: which findings matter enough to lead with, what context the exec needs that isn't in the data, and any number close enough to a threshold to need a second look before it goes out.

Data governance & quality

Why it matters more with AI in the loop

An AI co-analyst is only as reliable as the data and confidence signals it's given — without governance, it will confidently narrate noise as a trend.

The core disciplines

Confidence thresholds on every automated insight, source attribution so a number can be traced back to its origin, and defined reliability tiers for signals that feed decisions vs. signals that are directional only.

Confidence thresholds & source attribution

Confidence thresholds

A minimum sample size, statistical significance level, or model confidence score an insight must clear before it's surfaced as actionable rather than "still gathering signal."

Source attribution

Every number in a report or briefing traces back to its query, dataset, and timestamp — so a stakeholder questioning a figure gets an answer, not "the AI said so."

Human validation before decisions

Decision stakesValidation bar
Directional read, internal discussionSpot-check the query and the trend direction
Reallocating spend across channelsConfirm sample size, check for confounds, cross-reference a second source
Brand or public-facing claimFull manual review — source, methodology, and significance all verified

The higher the stakes of the decision an insight feeds, the more validation it needs before it's trusted — governance scales with consequence, not with how confident the AI sounds.

Attribution models

ModelCredit given to
Last-click100% to the final touchpoint before conversion
First-click100% to the touchpoint that started the journey
LinearEqual credit split across every touchpoint
Position-basedHeavier weight to first and last touch, remainder split across the middle
Time-decayMore credit to touchpoints closer in time to the conversion

Rule-based models are simple and explainable, but every one of them encodes an assumption about which touchpoint mattered — none of them derive it from the data.

Data-Driven Attribution (DDA)

How it differs

DDA uses machine learning to compare converting and non-converting paths and assign credit based on each touchpoint's actual, observed contribution to conversion probability — not a fixed rule.

The tradeoff

More accurate when there's enough conversion volume to train on, but a black box compared to a rule-based model — and still limited to the touchpoints the platform can actually observe.

The return of Marketing Mix Modeling

Why it's back

Cookie deprecation, app tracking transparency, and walled-garden data limits mean click-level attribution increasingly can't see the whole path — MMM uses aggregate, privacy-safe spend and outcome data instead of individual-level tracking.

What it adds

Statistical modeling of spend vs. outcomes across channels over time, capturing offline and brand channels attribution can't touch, plus diminishing-returns curves for budget planning.

Modern practice pairs MMM's macro view with DDA's micro view — not one replacing the other.

Best practices

1. Write the measurement plan before any GTM tagging work starts
2. Keep the dataLayer contract as the agreed spec between engineering and analytics
3. Export to BigQuery early — it's the foundation for every AI-assisted analysis step
4. Treat AI-generated SQL and narratives as a first draft, always checked against the original question
5. Set confidence thresholds before an insight is allowed to reach a dashboard or briefing
6. Keep source attribution on every automated number so it can be traced and defended
7. Scale human validation to the stakes of the decision, not to how confident the output sounds
8. Pick an attribution model that matches the business question, not just the platform default
9. Don't trust DDA output below the conversion volume needed to train it reliably
10. Pair MMM's macro view with DDA's micro view instead of picking one measurement system
